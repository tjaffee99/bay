# Bay Area Through Time — project brief

An interactive 3D web map of the San Francisco Bay Area where you can pan, zoom, tilt, and drag a year slider (1769 → today) to see what the region looked like in any given year: buildings, shoreline, marshes, orchards, freeways, bridges.

Visual inspiration: the "Latos Charts" YouTube channel (e.g. "BERLIN - 3D Timelapse"): extruded flat-shaded building blocks, low-poly trees, a year counter and population counter, serif era titles — but interactive in the browser.

**Owner's standing instructions (2026-11):**
- Landmarks (Golden Gate Bridge, Legion of Honor, Salesforce Tower/Park, Transamerica Pyramid, Painted Ladies, stadiums, parks, …) must be **actual reconstructions** from real dimensions and photos, not procedural stand-ins.
- Use real data and real reference imagery **as much as possible**; procedural generation **as little as possible**. When something must be generated, mark it `year_confidence = procedural` and prefer tracing a real historical map over inventing geometry.

## Core idea

Every feature is a geometry with a lifespan:

```
{ geometry, kind, height_m, start_year, end_year, year_confidence, source }
```

* `kind`: building | landmark | land | water | marsh | fill | orchard | forest | road | rail | freeway | bridge | wall …
* `end_year`: `9999` if it still exists.
* `year_confidence`: `exact` (from records) | `estimated` (imputed) | `procedural` (generated to match a historical map) | `reconstructed` (hand-modelled landmark from cited dimensions).
* `source`: short id pointing to an entry in `SOURCES.md`.

The viewer shows a feature iff `start_year <= year < end_year`.

## Running

```
make setup      # python venv + npm install
make sf         # download + build San Francisco data (~2 min after downloads)
make terrain    # elevation + land-cover tiles for the whole Bay Area (~20 min, ~80 MB, not committed)
make dev        # viewer at http://localhost:5173
make deploy     # build + publish viewer/dist (incl. terrain tiles) to gh-pages → bay.theojaffee.net
```

Pipeline stages (each idempotent, cached in data/raw or data/interim):
`download_sf` → `land` → `fire_1906` → `sf_join` → `sf_buildings` → `sf_predecessors` → `sf_export`, plus `download_overture` (buildings, parts, base land/land_use, roads, freeways, rail), `trees` (street trees), `ground` (parks, woods, sand), `transit` (BART + Muni tracks, stations, service lines), `roads` (streets, region roads, historic routes), `ground_detail` (golf/sport/park outlines + park labels), and the terrain chain `baylands_pdf` → `terrain_grid` → `terrain_tiles`.

## Landmarks (viewer/src/landmarks/)

Hand-modelled low-poly reconstructions built at runtime with a small mesh kit (`kit.ts`: prisms, lofts, domes, gables, hips, beams, parabolic cables, ribbons). Each landmark (`types.ts`) has `built: [construction start, completion]` (the model rises over that span), `end`, an anchor, cited `facts`/`sources`, and optional `replaces` polygons — the generic footprint extrusions under a *standing* landmark are hidden while landmarks are shown, and older buildings on the site stand until the landmark's construction starts (ground-detail fields at stadium sites are dated by the stadium).
All dimensions come from `data/research/landmarks.md` (sources per item); anything estimated says so in the model's facts ("Model: …").

## Decisions log (deviations from the original brief, with reasons)

1. **No modern basemap.** MapLibre runs with an empty style (background = water colour). Modern streets/labels/shoreline would be anachronistic. Land is our own polygon (TIGER shoreline for now; time-varying in Phase 2). MapLibre still provides camera, controls, and later terrain.
2. **No tiles for San Francisco.** SF (~177k buildings) ships as one compact columnar binary (`BAYT` format, ~9 MB gzip) that loads once and stays on the GPU: no feature dropping at low zoom (pitfall 7 doesn't arise), and the slider never waits on tile loads. PMTiles returns at Phase 5 (9 counties, ~3M buildings) together with the low-zoom aggregate.
3. **Own `TimeExtension` instead of `DataFilterExtension`.** Same GPU technique (visibility computed in the vertex shader from a `[start,end]` attribute, slider only changes a uniform) but one attribute instead of two, and it also drives the grow-in/sink animation and the "scorched" tint for buildings ending (e.g. 1906). Works on polygons (`mode: 'polygon'`) and meshes (`'mesh-z'`, `'mesh'`, `'fade'`).
4. **Triangulation in a Web Worker.** The binary stores delta-coded integer coordinates + ring-end markers (byte-shuffled for gzip); the worker decodes and earcuts (~1–2 s) so the file stays small.
5. **Geometry precedence (real before generic):** OSM hand-modelled `building_part`s (via Overture) > DataSF 2016 footprints > Overture/OSM footprints for buildings newer than the 2016 snapshot. Hand-reconstructed landmark meshes replace all of those for named landmarks.
6. **Heights:** 2010 LiDAR median height from the DataSF footprints for buildings that existed in 2010; stories × 3.3 m + 1.5 m for newer ones; then OSM/Overture height; then 7 m. **Stepped massing** (`pipeline/lidar_parts.py`, 2023 USGS 3DEP point cloud): footprints with several roof levels (towers over podiums, set-backs, wings) are split into parts at their measured heights (`sf_buildings.apply_lidar_parts`, ~3.7k footprints → ~8.7k parts); OSM building parts win unless they share one height or disagree with the LiDAR by > 6 m; parts of one footprint share its ground (`lidar_group`) and material. In the region, the downtowns of Oakland, Berkeley, Emeryville, San José, Walnut Creek, Palo Alto, San Mateo, Redwood City and Mountain View run the same tool on the 2018–21 3DEP surveys (`lidar_parts.py region`): split footprints become parts, and single-level footprints take their measured height where they stood at the survey (Overture often has none).
7. **Missing SF years are imputed from neighbours** (median of nearest exact-year buildings within 150 m, respecting bounds), not ACS — ACS returns for Phase 5 (Census API now requires a key).
8. **Population counter:** zoomed in (≥ 11.5), the city / town / CDP under the centre of the map (TIGER 2024 outlines, `pipeline/cities.py` → `cities.json`, census series in `data/tables/population_places.csv`), else its county; (no figure, rather than San Francisco's early estimates, over a place outside the city before its first count); zoomed out, the total of the mapped counties (ten since 2026-11; the label is derived: "Bay Area (10 counties)"); before 1850 the early San Francisco estimates.
9. **Trees:** street trees are real (DataSF positions; planting year recorded or estimated from trunk diameter). Outside SF, ~710k street / park trees from 20 city, county and campus inventories (`pipeline/region_trees.py`, z12 tiles `region-trees/` loaded from zoom 12.5 and joined per form into three instanced layers): recorded planting dates where plausible (bulk data-entry dates rejected), else age from DBH by the USDA Urban Tree Database growth equations, never more than 3 years before the street's buildings or before the ground existed (native oaks / bays / sycamores ≥ 30 in keep their age as remnants). Park woods use real OSM wood extents with procedural positions inside (no per-tree survey exists) — confidence `procedural`. Woods are dated by park year, Golden Gate Park's documented east→west reclamation, the 1886 Presidio planting, and Lands End's 1930s planting.
10. **Built data in git:** `viewer/public/data/sf-buildings.bin.gz` and friends are committed at milestones so a fresh clone runs with `npm install && npm run dev`; otherwise regenerate with `make sf`.

11. **Terrain = one mesh for land, water and land cover.** MapLibre terrain was not used: deck.gl's own `TerrainMeshLayer` (Web-Mercator tiles, 64×64 cells, region grid z13 ≈ 60 m from 3DEP overview 1, detail grid z14 ≈ 30 m over SF and the urban bay plain — Richmond / San Rafael to San José, the Peninsula, the East Bay to Fremont, Walnut Creek and the Tri-Valley; decorations stay on the z13 lattice the farm shader uses) carries per-vertex *cover histories* — a natural c.1800 cover plus up to 4 dated changes (fill, diking, farms/orchards, city, 1906 burn, parks/woods). The year is a uniform, so the slider never reloads tiles; covers blend over 2.5 years (the burn is instant; water turning to land takes half a year, so a building never stands on half-blue ground). Shoreline over time falls out of this: bay cells become fill/city at their researched year. Drawing: the vertex grid is 30–60 m, so covers are NOT interpolated as vertex colours (blurry squares) nor snapped to vertices (staircases); each tile keeps a small texture of its current cover colours (rebuilt on the CPU when the year moves) and the fragment shader snaps a cubic B-spline sample of it to the nearest real cover with a little noise — crisp, rounded, organic edges and coastlines. Hills are smooth-shaded from per-vertex normals.
12. **c.1800 cover, best source wins:** WorldCover-based rules < SFEI EcoAtlas poster (vectorised from the PDF, georeferenced by label control points + IoU refinement + local rubber-sheet) < SFEI historical-ecology GIS < Hidden Nature SF. SFEI c.1998 "fill" is bay by definition (Treasure Island is water until 1936).
13. **Trees and farms are procedural on real evidence:** trees stand on real WorldCover tree pixels (forest/oak/riparian/park covers, species by region and elevation); orchard rows and farmsteads are generated in dated farm/orchard cells. Trees are drawn ~2× real crown size so they read at map scale (Latos style). Confidence `procedural`.
13b. **Before 1850:** no farm cover, paddocks or farmsteads before American settlement — the mission / rancho range is open grassland (`landcover_history.csv` scv_mission_rancho_grazing), farmsteads start ≥ 1849; contact-era Native villages (`data/manual/ohlone/`, `early_region.csv`, `early_sf.csv`) are tule houses drawn at 2× their 3–5 m size (like the trees) and labelled (`village` tier, `ground_detail.village_labels`). Footprints that are not buildings above ground (plazas on underground garages: Portsmouth Square, St. Mary's Square) are dropped via `data/tables/not_buildings.csv`.
14. **Pre-1906 city (Phase 3) is procedural massing on real lots:** SF parcel lines mostly date from the 19th-century subdivisions, so `sf_predecessors.py` stands era-typical buildings on today's lots wherever the researched extent snapshot says the block was built (massing per `sf_extent_README.md` rules: gold-rush 1–2 storey frame before 1853, replaced downtown by 2–5 storey brick in the 1850s; 3–6 storeys after 1890; 2–3 storey houses elsewhere), ending them in the fire or when today's building replaced them. Documented buildings (`notable_pre1906.csv`) take their lots. Sanborn tracing would replace this block by block.
15. **Roads are draped paths, not polygons:** DataSF centerlines (SF, dated by extent snapshot or first nearby building), Overture roads elsewhere, researched routes (cable car, streetcar, Key System, ferries, demolished freeways) with lifespans. **Freeways and ramps that still exist come from Overture's real geometry (`freeways.bin.gz`)** with heights from its level/bridge flags (approaches ≤ 5.5 %, junctions continuous, ramp ends meeting streets at grade); the researched route lines only date them (only routes that still exist date today's geometry; `mode = bridge` rows included; see decision 41). Elevated stretches get concrete deck slabs with parapets and bents sized to the real height; the Bay Bridge / Golden Gate decks belong to their landmark models. Cars and trams move on the GPU along straight legs of their roads with an era density curve (none before ~1903) — `procedural`.
16. **Labels** (`places.csv`) show the name in use that year, by zoom tier, with a collision filter; neighborhood names change with the era (e.g. Yerba Buena → San Francisco 1847, South of the Slot, Butchertown). Parks that were renamed carry a name history in `data/tables/park_names.csv` (one row per name period, source per row; region rows matched by name + lon/lat): `ground_detail.py` emits one park label per period, never before the park (`parks.csv`) — Lobos Square → Funston Playground 1922 → Moscone 1978, North Beach Playground → Joe DiMaggio 2000; a park without a documented name in a period gets no label then.
17. **UI:** no era title card, no layer toggles (landmarks and trees are always on); `?debug` shows the data-quality overlay and event jump; shift+drag rotates/tilts. **Story mode (2026-11):** the `Story` button (or `?story`, `?story=N` from stop N) plays a guided tour — `data/tables/story.csv` (id, year, year_end, camera, orbit, title, text, source, note, effect, effect_year, dwell_s; 92 stops, ~27 minutes; chronological with `year` non-decreasing, about half in San Francisco; buildings, events and people — Summer of Love, Milk / Moscone, Angel Island, Port Chicago, Silicon Valley …; every stop anchored at the real place and backed by data the map shows). Owner's rules for the script: American dates and spelling ("June 14, 1846"), captions that stand on their own for someone who knows no Bay Area history (who, what, why it matters; 2–3 sentences), a pace you can just read. `viewer/src/story.ts` only moves the camera and the year: it starts at the stop whose years contain or follow the year on screen (from the beginning when the map shows today), flies to a stop (2.6–5.5 s, low arc, the caption already up, the year moving in steps while tiles load) while the year runs to the stop's year, holds for a reading time (2 s + 50 ms per character, 6–16 s, or the stop's `dwell_s`) while the camera turns and the year runs on to `year_end`, and pauses on any drag, wheel, touch, click or slider input, or when the tab is hidden; space / arrows / Esc and the card's buttons control it. `effect = quake` stops shake the view (CSS on the map container + building sway in the shader) when the year passes `effect_year`; fires and collapses need no column (decision 25). `?storyspeed=N` runs it N times faster for headless checks (software GL draws a frame in tens of seconds to minutes: the check steps through the stops with the → key; a 520 × 340 screenshot takes ~4 minutes). A stop about something that vanishes runs across its end date, so table end years that drive a stop are fractional (Cypress Viaduct 1989.79, the documented buildings lost on April 18, 1906 = 1906.29), not whole years.
18. **Performance rules (2026-11 pass; keep them):** deck.gl's per-layer cost dominates frames, so
    * few layers: all landmarks are ONE merged mesh (`landmarks/merged-layer.ts`, time/grow in the shader, per-vertex picking colour); terrain decorations of all visible tiles are concatenated into one instanced `DecoLayer` per kind (`layers/deco-layer.ts`), street trees and viaduct columns use the same layer; region roads are split into an 8×8 grid and only squares near the view are drawn;
    * nothing rebuilds per animation frame: traffic moves in the vertex shader from a time uniform (`TrafficLayer`), the loop only calls `setNeedsRedraw()`; the terrain reads the year at draw time; labels are decluttered on the CPU in screen space only on moveend / zoom step / year (no GPU collision pass);
    * level of detail: trees use 4–10-triangle meshes below zoom 15 and are thinned below 14 (`treeLod`);
    * no hover picking (`pickAsync: false`), objects are picked on click; deck canvas capped at 2× device pixels, MapLibre at 1×; the high-performance GPU is requested, and a banner warns when the browser renders WebGL in software.
    * nothing blinks when zooming (2026-11): tiled layers (region buildings, suburban streets) and big static layers (ground detail, region trees, airport lines) stay in the layer list at every zoom — hidden with deck.gl `visibleMinZoom` / `visible`, loading a little before they are drawn — instead of being removed at a zoom threshold (which threw their tiles / tessellation away and reloaded them on the way back); trees and farm buildings carry a stable rank (position hash in the colour alpha) and thin continuously with the zoom read in the shader (`project.scale`), never by instance index; terrain tiles use `refinementStrategy: 'no-overlap'` (two zoom levels of opaque mesh z-fight); z13 terrain tiles over the detail grid carry the detail grid's decorations (`merge_detail_deco`) so trees don't swap at zoom 12.5; open-ocean tiles the pipeline skips are synthesised as flat water in the viewer (the background is parchment since decision 32).
    * things lying on the terrain (ground detail, runways / aprons) use `DrapeExtension` (`layers/drape-extension.ts`): depth from a point pulled 0.4 % of its distance toward the camera (screen position unchanged), no depth write — they never sink under the mesh or z-fight when zoomed out or on the far side of a pitched view, and buildings / trees drawn after still cover them; no vertical lift. Their pipelines sort polygons in stacking order (lawns → fields → greens → bunkers; aprons → taxiways → runways).
    * published size (2026-11): GitHub Pages cancels a deployment whose file sync takes more than ten minutes — the 483 MB ten-county build failed three times, 365 MB went through. Terrain tiles therefore don't ship decorations the tile loader never draws (`terrain_tiles.prune_unseen_deco`: the loader's own rank test, same hash, applied in the pipeline — 24 M of 47 M tree records; keep it in step with `decodeTile`). Still four fifths of the tiles are tree records: generating forest trees in the browser from a tree-cover mask would cut the tiles to a fraction (not done).
    * animation without stutter (2026-11, story mode: owner "super slow and laggy and janky"): terrain tiles are fetched, unpacked and decoded in workers (`layers/terrain-worker.ts` runs the WebGL-free `terrain-tile.ts`; a tile with 27k trees took 30–200 ms on the main thread, dozens arrive during a flight); a terrain tile recomputes and re-uploads its cover texture only when the year leaves the span over which its ground is unchanged (`coverColors` returns it); the merged tree buffers are rebuilt at most every 0.9 s while the camera travels; San Francisco's building and street-tree buffers are not drawn (`visible: false`, kept on the GPU) while the city is out of view; and an adaptive-resolution governor (main.ts) drops the deck canvas from 2× to 1.5× to 1× device pixels after a run of frames slower than ~30 fps while something animates, and restores it when the map is still (`?dpr=N` fixes it). Headless profiling cannot measure the GPU side (software GL spends 35 s a frame inside buffer uploads): judge by reasoning and by the owner's report.
    * one draw of the scene per frame, and less in it (2026-11, owner: "make rendering faster"): the MapboxOverlay drew the whole scene twice in every frame in which the camera moved while anything animated (once from MapLibre's `render` event, once from deck's own animation loop) — `main.ts` wraps `deck._drawLayers` so the second call of a frame only leaves the redraw flag set; street and park trees use the 4–10-triangle meshes below zoom 16.6 (was 15: 274k full trees were 39 M vertices in a downtown view) and cars are one box below the same zoom; San Francisco's buildings are 48 chunks (`chunkBinary`: 16 k-d blocks of equal count × three era classes — gone by 1906.7, built from 1946, the rest) and only the chunks in view and alive in the year on screen are drawn (`visibleChunks`; region tiles stay whole). Measured with the draw calls stubbed (`scratch story/cpuprof.cjs` pattern: count vertices per layer in `Layer._drawLayer`): downtown at zoom 15.4 went from 92 M to 14.2 M vertices and from ~115 to 75 draw calls per frame. Next candidates if it is still slow: `ground-detail-region` and the route / street paths are drawn whole (1.2–1.7 M vertices each).
    * WebGPU was considered and rejected for now: deck.gl's WebGPU backend is experimental and lacks text, paths and our GLSL shader injections; the bottlenecks were CPU-side, not the graphics API.

19. **What stood before today's buildings (2026-11 pass):**
    * exempt / institutional buildings (hospitals, campuses, public housing, schools, piers) have no assessor year: `known_building_dates.csv` (1,076 researched rows); tall buildings without a usable year get height floors (≥35 m → ≥1920, ≥50 m → ≥1950; towers missing from the 2016 snapshot → ≥2014);
    * urban renewal and freeway clearances (`data/manual/redevelopment_areas.csv`, 19 areas): imputed buildings can't predate the clearance; the old fabric (Victorian rows, residential hotels, warehouses) ends there, and superblock parcels get rows of era-typical buildings along their street edges (procedural);
    * demolished post-1906 buildings (`notable_demolished.csv`: Geneva Towers, Fox Theatre, Winterland, SP depot, lost piers…), the town before the gold rush (`early_sf.csv`: Ohlone villages, the Mission compound and village, ranchos, Yerba Buena 1835–49; generated fabric starts in 1849), the 1915 and 1939 expositions and other lost landmarks (`landmarks/batch4.ts`, traced from the 1914 Sanborn map of the PPIE);
    * nothing stands before its ground: buildings, trees and roads on bay fill are moved to the year the ground appeared (terrain land-year grid); generated buildings follow era massing rules; 19th-century cemeteries (Laurel Hill, Calvary, Odd Fellows, Masonic) are open green until the graves were moved.
20. **Facade materials** (`sf_export.materials`): Presidio 1890s barracks red brick, its 1909–1945 Army buildings white stucco under red clay tile (like Fort Mason); painted-wood Victorians, stucco Sunset/Marina (with clay-tile roofs), brick, terra cotta / granite downtown, bronze and glass towers, contemporary panels, adobe and tule for the early town; roofs (flat, tile, shingle) are drawn on the top faces only.

21. **Build-out without "pop-in" (2026-11):** a neighbourhood's ground turns from field to city when its buildings go up (2nd-earliest building, incl. vanished ones, within ~60 m of each terrain cell; the extent snapshots only fill gaps), streets are cut a year before the first buildings along them, and generated predecessors are block-coherent (a growth front from the previous extent snapshot, so whole blocks fill together) instead of a random speckle.
22. **Ground detail (satellite look):** golf fairways/greens/tees/bunkers, pitches, running tracks, playgrounds, plazas and gardens are real OSM outlines (Overture `land_use`) draped 0.3 m above the terrain (`ground-detail.bin.gz`), dated by course / park / first buildings nearby. Every dated city park is labelled (`park-labels.json`).
23. **Look (2026-11, reverted):** a satellite-style restyle (aerial palette, procedural window grids and roof materials, ground noise, sidewalks, era street surfaces, dark tree greens) was tried and reverted at the owner's request — procedural facade detail made buildings look less accurate up close. The map keeps the earlier palette and flat-shaded buildings. Sun shadows exist but are opt-in (`?shadow`, zoom >= 13.5, cached shadow map; layers with `shadowEnabled: false` and their sublayers are skipped in the shadow pass).
24. **BART and Muni (`pipeline/transit.py`, `layers/transit.ts`):** tracks are Overture/OSM rail (each track its own line, aerial structures with decks and bents, tunnels as a faint line), service lines are BART GTFS shapes and SFMTA route patterns re-centred between their tracks, stations from GTFS / DataSF with researched opening days. Trains are procedural but to scale: real car lengths and train lengths, trapezoid speed profile with dwell at each stop, constant headway per line; they run entirely in the vertex shader (line vertices + stop knots in a float texture), keep right, follow the tracks round island platforms, vanish on stretches not yet open and while they run underground (no x-ray drawing). Muni's streetcar/cable car history before 1980 stays in `routes.csv` (roads.py ends those lines in 1980 and the modern lines take over).
25. **Construction and destruction on screen (2026-11; owner: "you should see buildings rising, going up; the earthquakes should show shaking, buildings collapsing, fires"):** a building goes up as its frame, floor by floor — timber studs/joists under 14 m, a concrete or steel frame with floor slabs above — over its construction period before `start_year` (≈ 4 months for a house, ~1 year at 40 m, up to 3 years for a tower; the frame tops out at 70 % of the time and the cladding follows it up from the ground; TimeExtension 'polygon' + building shader), and stands finished from `start_year`. Landmarks are cut off at the height the work has reached between `built[0]` and `built[1]` (frame, then cladding; bridges in unpainted steel). Ordinary demolition still sinks and darkens over the months before `end_year`. **Disasters** (`time-extension.ts` `DISASTERS`: May 1851 fire, April 18, 1906, Berkeley 1923, Loma Prieta, Oakland Hills 1991, Tubbs 2017, Kincade, the 2020 complexes, Glass): a building or landmark whose end year is the event's stands until the event, then ignites in patches a few hundred metres across over the event's real duration, burns (charred walls, a flickering flame-coloured roof line) and falls — the year is passed to the shader as "years since the event" so days resolve. The end years in the data must be the event's (1906.29, 1923.71, 1991.80, 2017.77 …), not whole years.
26. **Freeways meet the ground:** ends that no other freeway segment uses (ramps and mainlines meeting a street), and tunnel portals, are pinned at grade; heights come down to them through the network (graph distance over Overture connectors): ramps blend from the mainline height to the street (≤ 8 % where long enough, steeper on short ramps so they never detach), mainline ends at 5 %, tunnel approaches at 5.5 %; junctions are made continuous afterwards. Dates: each mainline segment takes the researched route running *parallel* to it (a crossing freeway at an interchange doesn't count), and each group of connected ramps opens with the latest mainline it joins — and not before the buildings standing where it runs are gone (taller than the road there; mainlines only when off by > 8 years). Drawing: Overture segments are chained end to end (connectors joining exactly two segments of the same kind and date) so a road is one continuous path, and ramp ends meeting a street get a last vertex on the street centreline. In SF the coarse region terrain tiles are capped by the 30 m detail grid (min-pooled) so roads in cuts (US-101 below Potrero Hill) aren't buried when zoomed out.

41. **Data audit (2026-11) — rules that came out of it (keep them):**
    * **Invariants, checked on the built files** (scripts: scratch `audit/lead/inv_*.py` pattern): no feature with start ≥ end; nothing before its ground; no building inside the 1906 burn dated before the fire unless a researched row says it survived; no two buildings on one spot at the same time (`sf_predecessors.resolve_overlaps`, also run for the counties: documented > traced > generated; a vanished building inside a footprint that already stood ends there or goes); documented survivors are not drawn twice; no year carrying thousands of buildings more than its neighbours.
    * **Placeholder and rounded years are spread, never stacked:** HISDAC first-built-up epochs over their 5 years and after; assessor heaps in the counties (1900, 1910, 1920, and Alameda's 1908 / 1912 …) over ± 2–5 years as `estimated` (`region_buildings.deheap`); a placeholder that caps a neighbour estimate spreads over the 8 years before it.
    * **Evidence order for a building's year:** researched row (never clamped by fire / cemetery / land bounds; a part of a split footprint takes the row whose point lies in it) > historic-resource survey (OHP, also in SF: `ohp_sf_years.csv`) > assessor > permit > neighbours. 8+ storey buildings without a usable year are post-fire; a LiDAR part 15 m taller than the 2010 building is a post-2010 addition; estimated buildings can't predate a clearance (SF and counties).
    * **Ground:** made land is dated from map editions (`fill_zones.csv` `tp_*` = bracket between the last edition showing water and the first showing land; `ot_*` = marsh edge under 19th-century houses); where recorded buildings predate a cell's fill year the ground dates from them (two against a researched zone, one against a fallback guess; `region_recorded_points.parquet` is written before the land check); marsh diked long before it was filled is DIKED in between; logged redwoods are brush until their second growth.

    * **Freeways:** only routes that still exist (`year_end = 9999`) date today's Overture geometry; `mode = bridge` rows are used; trunk roads without research are dated like main roads; nothing outside the map extent is written. A freeway segment at the 1960 default is a missing `routes.csv` row, not a date.
    * **Landmarks:** a footprint standing today inside a standing landmark's `replaces` outline is the landmark (hidden in every year); the run of models on one site hides generated buildings from its first model (`chainStart`); a landmark that is gone clears its site while it stood (`occupiedMask`). `replaces` outlines follow the building's own outline, not its block.
    * **Viewer:** the slider ends at the end of the current year (buildings finished this year appear). The attribution line's Sources link opens `sources.html`, which `viewer/scripts/sources_html.mjs` renders from `SOURCES.md` on `npm run dev` / `build` (not committed; the repository has no `main` branch to link to).
    * **Follow-up rules (2026-11):** an SF footprint without a usable year that the 2023 LiDAR shows ≥ 15 m high and 8 m above anything the 2010 survey saw there, or a sizeable building only OSM / Overture has (not in the 2010-based DataSF footprints), is newer than 2010 (`lidar_new` / `after_2010_survey`, estimated inside 2012–2021); the forest / oak-woodland split of the natural cover follows the fog belt (distance from the outer coast + elevation, with noise), never a meridian; an orchard tree goes when its own nearest terrain vertex stops being farm; each terrain cell keeps three farm eras (first, the one the last replaced, last); a reserve row never turns ground that was bay or marsh back to its natural cover (only a `restored` fill row does); reservoirs are dated from the National Inventory of Dams; renamed BART stations are labelled with the name in use (`data/tables/transit_station_names.csv`).

    * **No political line in the landscape.** The natural cover of the northern peninsula (San Francisco and south over Colma and San Bruno Mountain to about South San Francisco, `terrain_tiles.north_peninsula`, southern edge wandering with noise) follows the ground where no c.1800 map exists: coastal scrub on steeper, north- and east-facing slopes and along the ocean, grassland elsewhere, no native forest (today's trees there are planted) — it used to be "inside the SF box = scrub", a ruler-straight seam at the county line. Farm eras inside San Francisco (truck gardens, dairies, nurseries: `data/manual/landscape/agriculture_sf.geojson`, same schema as `agriculture.geojson`) are painted like the districts south of the line.
    * **Landmarks stand on the mesh.** `pipeline/landmark_ground.py` (`make landmark-ground`; anchors and reach of every model dumped by `viewer/scripts/landmark_anchors.mjs`) writes full-resolution elevation patches round every landmark (`elevation-spots.bin.gz`); `elevationAt` reads them first. The 240 m lookup had Fort Ross 8 m, Lick Observatory 21–35 m inside the hill. Rerun after the terrain grids change or landmarks are added or moved.
    * **Bay Bridge** rebuilt on its real alignment and measured heights (owner: "the shape is wack" — the old model was a flat deck on guessed points, the 1936 span on the wrong side of today's; see the model's facts and `data/research/landmarks.md`); landmark models can be previewed offline with `viewer/scripts/landmark_preview.mjs` (software rasteriser, seconds per view — headless WebGL screenshots take minutes).

44. **Shipyards, works and camps that are gone (2026-11, owner: "fix the missing Kaiser shipyards and anything similar"):** a site of decision 38 carried its ground and a label, and its footprints standing today were dated — but what stood on it and is gone was missing, so a wartime yard was bare ground. `data/manual/industrial_sites/tools/trace_vanished.py` (targets in `tools/targets.py`) traces the building symbols of the first 1:24,000 USGS sheet after a site's founding inside its outline (solid blocks; large buildings printed as a dot screen are found by the density of detached dots; rails, road casings, tanks, lettering and symbols in the red built-up tint are left out), drops the symbols that are a footprint of today (unless today's building there is younger than the map — then the symbol is its predecessor), and dates the rest from the editions: back to the last sheet that does not draw it (else the site's founding), forward to the first that does not (end = between the two, or the site's closing year + 0.5–3.5 years when that lies between). `vanished_buildings.geojson`: 573 buildings at 18 sites — Kaiser's four Richmond yards (138; the 1947 sheet from 1946 photos shows them as they closed), Marinship, Moore Dry Dock, Selby smelter, Giant and Hercules powder works, Oakland Army Base (174), Camp Parks (122), Camp Stoneman, Skaggs Island, Bayshore yard, the Oakland Mole — read by `region_predecessors.py` like the vanished-site buildings (`source = vanished_site`, `estimated`; heights are one tall storey by area, ESTIMATED). The Kaiser outlines were wrong and are redrawn from the same sheet (Yard 1 at the head of the Harbor Channel was missing, Yard 3 was an 8 ha polygon north of Point Potrero, Yard 4 is new; `tools/build.py <site> --merge` rewrites single sites); Yard 3's surviving 1942 buildings are dated 1942 by the editions. Not done: the shipways and graving basins as models, Port Chicago's town and piers (Concord NWS), Schlage Lock (in San Francisco, which has its own tables), slanted dot screens (two warehouses north of the Ford plant are missed).

## Year cleaning (SF assessor roll)

* raw year = most common non-zero `year_property_built` among the assessor records of the footprint's map parcel (ties → earliest).
* **spike years** are detected automatically: a multiple of 5 before 1950 whose count is > 4× the median of nearby non-round years. On the 2025 roll this flags 1875, 1880, 1885, 1890, 1895, **1900** (14.6k buildings vs ~400/yr around it). Spike years are placeholders: imputed with an upper bound of the spike year.
* **burned**: raw year < 1906 inside the 1906 burned area (minus unburned islands) is impossible — imputed with a lower bound of 1906.5.
* **land_not_yet**: a building can't predate its land (`data/manual/land_not_before.geojson`; Phase 2 generalises this to all fill).
* imputation: median of up to 12 nearest good-year buildings within 150 m that satisfy the bounds; last resort draws from the empirical distribution (spikes) or a documented range. Imputed rows → `estimated`.
* sub-year jitter (deterministic hash) so a whole tract doesn't appear on the same frame.
* Report: `data/interim/reports/sf_years.png`, `sf_buildings_summary.json`.

## Known pitfalls (read before building)

1. The 1906 earthquake and fire destroyed ~28,000 buildings in SF. Assessor years < 1906 inside the burned zone are wrong; outside it (much of the Mission west of the fire line, Haight, parts of the Western Addition) pre-1906 Victorians survive.
2. Assessor year values are messy: spikes at round years, zeros/nulls. Plot the histogram first; treat spikes as `estimated`.
3. Assessor data is per parcel (per unit for condos), not per building. Group by map parcel; multi-building parcels share one year.
4. Today's ground isn't yesterday's ground: Financial District over Yerba Buena Cove, the Marina (1915 Expo), Mission Bay, Treasure Island (1936–37), Foster City, SFO, Bay Farm Island, much of the East Bay waterfront. A building's `start_year` must never precede its land's.
5. Demolished things don't exist in modern data: Embarcadero Freeway (1959–1991), Central Freeway, Cypress Structure (1957–1989), Key System, Western Addition/SoMa redevelopment clearances, old Bay Bridge east span (to 2013), Playland, Sutro Baths, Candlestick Park, Seals Stadium, … — add manually with cited dates.
6. Santa Clara Valley orchards (~100,000 acres at the 1930s–40s peak) are a first-class layer.
7. Tile simplification drops features at low zoom — use an aggregated low-zoom representation when tiling (Phase 5).
8. Licenses: OSM is ODbL (attribution + share-alike on derived databases — our built building file mixes OSM parts, so it is offered under ODbL); DataSF datasets are PDDL; check each county's terms. Keep attribution visible in the viewer.

## Working conventions

* Commit after each working step; keep the viewer runnable.
* Every pipeline stage is idempotent and cached; raw downloads are never modified in place.
* When a historical fact or date drives data, cite the source in `SOURCES.md` or next to the data.
* When unsure whether a year is real or a placeholder, mark it `estimated`.
* Prefer small, inspectable intermediates (GeoJSON/FlatGeobuf/Parquet) for QGIS.
* Screenshots: `cd viewer && node scripts/shots.cjs http://localhost:5173/ <outdir> <spec.json>` (headless Chromium; software GL is slow, ~30 s/frame with all of SF).
