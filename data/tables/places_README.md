# places.csv — era-accurate place-name labels

One row per **name period** of a place (583 rows: 490 after the 2026-11 audit + 93 for the ten-county extension, see the last section). A label is shown iff
`year_start <= year < year_end` (same rule as features; `9999` = still in use).

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | stable id, prefixed by area (`sf_`, `ala_`, `cc_`, `mar_`, `napa_`, `sol_`, `son_`, `smc_`, `scl_`, `scz_`, `reg_`) |
| `name` | label text as used in that period |
| `tier` | `region` (bays, counties, Washington Township) · `city` (incorporated cities, major historic settlements) · `town` (towns, villages, missions, unincorporated places) · `neighborhood` · `historic` (vanished/short-lived names, Ohlone villages, ranchos, expositions, shipyards) · `landscape` (hills, islands, points, creeks, straits) |
| `lat`,`lon` | WGS84 label anchor, 5 decimals |
| `year_start` | first year the name was in use for the place (founding, naming, subdivision, post office or grant — `note` says which) |
| `year_end` | first year it was no longer used (rename, annexation, demolition); `9999` = current |
| `replaced_by` | `id` of the next name for the same place. Usually `year_end` of the old row = `year_start` of the new one; deliberate gaps exist (Japantown 1942→1945 forced removal, Barbary Coast 1917→Jackson Square 1971, Laurel Hill Cemetery 1941→Laurel Heights 1946) |
| `county` | present-day county (blank for bay-wide regions) |
| `note` | what the dates mean; **"estimated"** marks any year not stated as such in the source (decade midpoints, inferred ends) — 123 rows |
| `source_url` | the page supporting the name and year (mostly Wikipedia; FoundSF, SF Planning, local history societies otherwise) |

## Conventions
* **Renames are chains**: e.g. `Yerba Buena` 1835–1847 → `San Francisco`; `San Antonio`/`Clinton` → `Brooklyn` 1856–1872 → `East Oakland`; `Ocean View` → `West Berkeley` 1878; `Union City` 1850 → `Alvarado` 1853 → `Union City` 1959; `McCarthysville` → `Bank Mills` → `Saratoga`; `Murphy's Station` → `Encinal` → `Sunnyvale` 1901; `School House Station` → `Colma` → `Lawndale` 1924 → `Colma` 1941; `Yerba Buena Island` → `Goat Island` 1895 → `Yerba Buena Island` 1931; `Loma Alta` → `Telegraph Hill` 1849.
* Towns that merged but whose name survives get two rows: `town` until the merger/annexation, then `neighborhood` (Fruitvale, Elmhurst, Temescal, Willow Glen, Alviso, Lorin). Fremont's five towns keep `town` tier to 9999.
* **Missions** end at secularization (c. 1834–36) and hand over to the town that grew around them.
* **Ranchos** (`historic`): `year_start` = grant year; `year_end` = 1852 (1853 where stated), the year the claim was filed with the US Public Land Commission under the Land Act of 1851 — a uniform cut-off for the rancho as a map label, cited in each rancho article.
* **Native villages** are no longer rows here: their labels come from the village tables (`early_sf.csv`, `early_region.csv`, `data/manual/ohlone/villages.csv` → `park-labels.json`, tier `village`) and end when each village emptied (1776–1820), not at a uniform 1800. Local tribes (Yelamu, Urebure …) stay as `historic` rows.
* **Natural features with no documented naming date** (Ocean Beach, Point Lobos, Candlestick Point, San Bruno Mountain, Carquinez Strait, Point Reyes, Farallones, Sweeney Ridge) use `year_start = 1769` so the landscape is labelled throughout; their notes say the first use is not established. Features with known naming dates use them (Golden Gate 1846, Tamalpais 1845, Mount Davidson 1911, Islais Creek 1834, …).
* Cities: `year_start` = founding/settlement/naming; incorporation is in `note`.

## Coordinates
Wikipedia article coordinates for cities, towns, landscape and ranchos (ranchos only to ~0.01°;
Rancho San Antonio re-anchored to central Oakland); OpenStreetMap place nodes from
`data/raw/osm_bbbike/SanFrancisco.osm.pbf` (ODbL) for most SF neighborhoods; DataSF Analysis
Neighborhoods centroid (PDDL, data.sfgov.org j2bu-swwd) for Lakeshore; hand-placed approximate
points for vanished places (Happy Valley, Tar Flat, Little Chile, Sydney Town, Harbor View,
Clinton, New Haven, Silveyville, villages) — their notes say so.

## Known gaps / caveats
* Not included for lack of a dated source: Lower Haight, Duboce Triangle, Presidio Heights, Polk Gulch, Lower Pacific Heights, India Basin; Dumbarton and Agnews; Ohlone tribelets other than the Yelamu (Huchiun, Tamien, Puichon, Lamchin…); most Oakland hill, Berkeley and San Jose neighborhoods (Rockridge, Montclair, North Berkeley, Berryessa, Evergreen, Alum Rock…).
* Conflicting sources, noted in `note`: Redwood City 1857 (county seat) vs 1867 (Britannica); Gilroy renamed 1868 vs incorporated 1870; Alvarado formed 1853 vs 1854; Yerba Buena 1834 (pueblo) vs 1835 (first dwelling, used here).
* Sub-area names (Inner/Outer Richmond) reuse the parent district's year; Inner/Outer Sunset use estimated development years.
* Research collected 2026-11 from web sources; verify before treating any single date as authoritative.

## Ten-county extension (2026-11)
The map now covers Sonoma, Napa, Solano and Santa Cruz counties and the parts of Contra Costa (Pittsburg, Antioch, Brentwood, the Delta) and Santa Clara (Morgan Hill, San Martin, Gilroy) that the core outline cut off. The table already held 67 rows there (written for the larger region); this pass added 93 rows and corrected 24.

* **Added (93)**: Sonoma 34 (Bodega 1853, Stumptown → Guerneville 1870, Los Guilicos → Kenwood, Geyserville, Forestville, Occidental, Duncans Mills, Cazadero, Monte Rio, Rio Nido, Camp Meeker, Jenner, The Sea Ranch 1965, Bloomfield, Valley Ford, Freestone, Fulton, Graton, Penngrove, El Verano, Boyes Hot Springs, Asti, Fort Ross after 1841, three ranchos, Bodega Bay, Russian River, Mount Saint Helena 1841, The Geysers, Lake Sonoma 1983, Sonoma Valley); Napa 10 (Oakville, Rutherford, Angwin, Berryessa Valley → Lake Berryessa 1957, Monticello 1866–1957, Knoxville 1863–1912, Rancho Caymus, Rancho Carne Humana, Napa Valley); Solano 10 (Brazos del Rio 1858–62, the first site of Rio Vista; Elmira; Collinsville → Newport 1867 → Collinsville 1872; Fairfield-Suisun Army Air Base 1942 → Travis AFB 1951; Rancho Los Putos; Suisun Marsh; Montezuma Hills); Santa Cruz 20 + Monterey Bay (Felton 1868, Boulder Creek, Ben Lomond 1887, Brookdale, Mount Hermon, Martinville → Glenwood 1880, Davenport Landing 1867 → Davenport 1906, Soquel, Aptos, Live Oak, Whiskey Hill → Freedom, Rob Roy 1925 → La Selva Beach 1935, two ranchos, Pajaro Valley, Loma Prieta); south Santa Clara 9 (Pleasant Valley → Gilroy, San Martin, Madrone –1959, three ranchos, Coyote Lake 1936, Anderson Lake 1950, Henry W. Coe State Park 1959); east Contra Costa 8 + the Delta (New York Landing, Rancho Los Méganos, Stewartville 1882–1902, Byron Hot Springs, Bethel Island, Franks Tract, Los Vaqueros Reservoir 1998, Clifton Court Forebay 1969).
* **Corrected name chains**: `New York of the Pacific` 1849 → `New York Landing` (c.1852, estimated) → `Black Diamond` **1903** (the town was incorporated and renamed by vote; before, the row started in 1859) → `Pittsburg` 1911; `Camp Capitola` **1874**–1895 (was 1869–1949) → `Capitola` (incorporated 1949; switch year estimated); `San Ysidro` moved to the site of Old Gilroy, 3 km east of the town, and `Pleasant Valley` 1850–68 added at today's downtown → `Gilroy` 1868; `Rio Vista` starts 1862 at its present site, the 1858–62 town at Cache Slough is `Brazos del Rio`; `Fort Ross` (historic, 1812–41) → `Fort Ross` (town, the ranch and landing after the Russians).
* **Anchors moved to the historic core** (Wikipedia's city point was 1–3 km off): Santa Rosa (Old Courthouse Square), Petaluma, Healdsburg (the Plaza), Cloverdale, Napa (First and Main), Vallejo (Georgia Street), Fairfield (courthouse), Suisun City (waterfront), Vacaville (Main Street), Dixon, Rio Vista, Watsonville (the Plaza), Capitola (the Esplanade), Gilroy, Morgan Hill (Monterey Road), Antioch (the landing), Pittsburg (Railroad Avenue). These were placed from knowledge of the old downtowns; they were **not** checked against pre-1925 building counts as the 2026-11 audit did, because the buildings of the new counties were not built yet - re-check once they are.
* **Estimated years** (flagged in `note`): the small towns whose article gives no founding year (Geyserville, Forestville, Cazadero, Monte Rio, Jenner, Valley Ford, Freestone, Graton, El Verano, Oakville, Elmira, Soquel, Aptos, San Martin, Madrone, Freedom, Kenwood, Glenwood's end, New York Landing, Pleasant Valley, Montezuma Hills, Napa Valley).
* **Not added**: Bodega Bay as a town, Birds Landing, Denverton, Maine Prairie, Rockville, Corralitos, Bonny Doon, Rio del Mar, Swanton, Lompico, Annapolis, Stewarts Point, Timber Cove, Eldridge, Schellville, Lakeville, Pope Valley, Deer Park, Sargent, Gilroy Hot Springs, Judsonville, West Hartley (no dated source in reach, or too small for the tiers); Roseland, Larkfield-Wikiup and other post-war subdivisions; an earlier name for American Canyon before its 1992 incorporation (Napa Junction is a different spot); Sherman Island and the other Delta islands of Sacramento and San Joaquin counties (outside the ten counties).
* **Native villages**: none added for the Coast Miwok, Pomo, Wappo, Patwin, Awaswas and Mutsun areas - no source giving village locations to the standard of `data/manual/ohlone/` was consulted in this pass (candidates: Barrett 1908 for Pomo / Wappo / Coast Miwok, Milliken 1995 and Kroeber 1925 for Awaswas and Mutsun; Metini at Fort Ross, Chitactac on Uvas Creek and Ewapalt at Valley Ford are documented sites).
